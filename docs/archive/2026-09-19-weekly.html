  1. 90E★An All-van-der-Waals Qubit
    Sein Park, Sameia Zaman, Junghyun Kim et al. · 2026-09-13 · watchlist: William Oliver👍 👎

    NbSe2-hBN-NbSe2 merged-element transmon reaches 55 us T1 and 21 us echo, matching Al-AlOx coherence in a far smaller footprint.

    Explain
    In plain words
    A transmon built entirely out of stacked atomically thin crystals held its quantum state about as long as the usual aluminium one.
    How it works
    A thin hBN flake sitting between two NbSe2 flakes is both the Josephson junction and the shunt capacitor, so the whole qubit is one small stack rather than a large aluminium pad; coherence is read out with ordinary circuit-QED pulses.
    Why it matters
    The same coherence in a much smaller footprint means far less stray capacitance to everything nearby, which is what limits how densely circuit elements can be packed.
    Be skeptical of
    Ramsey time is only 1.9 us because the device is unusually charge-noise sensitive, and these are a few hand-stacked devices, not a fabrication process.
  2. 88ELarge-scale quantum simulations of dissipative spin-1/2 Heisenberg chains
    João C. Getelina, Andrew Cox, Muhammad Asaduzzaman et al. · 2026-09-14👍 👎

    Lindblad dynamics of 50-site chains on 100 active ibm_kingston qubits at 1700-gate depth maps the dissipative phase diagram from 117 hardware points.

    Explain
    In plain words
    A 100-qubit processor was used to compute the steady states an open spin chain settles into, and produced the phase diagram that competing classical methods had been disagreeing about.
    How it works
    Chains of up to 50 sites evolve under Lindblad dynamics, with the environment faked by ancilla qubits and resets (Stinespring dilation), at up to 1700 entangling gates deep; structure factors at 117 parameter points map out the phases.
    Why it matters
    The dissipation is self-correcting, so hardware noise enters only as a weak extra bath rather than wrecking the run: engineered-dissipation problems are a far friendlier target for today's chips than unitary dynamics at this depth.
    Be skeptical of
    Correctness rests on that self-correction argument and on agreement between methods, not on an independent check at 50 sites; the claim to have settled the phase diagram is the authors'.
  3. 87TESpacetime mitigation of logical errors
    Laurin E. Fischer, Ali Javadi-Abhari, Simon Martiel et al. · 2026-09-11👍 👎

    Pairing error detection with first-order PEC on ibm_aachen cuts sampling overhead 63x over six Trotter steps, using 22 data and 27 check qubits.

    Explain
    In plain words
    They combined discarding runs that trip an error flag with a scheme that cancels the errors the flags miss, and got the same answer from 63 times fewer shots.
    How it works
    A spacetime noise model builds the leftover post-selected noise from the physical noise plus the measured syndromes, to first order; the post-selected maps are kept unnormalised so they can be cancelled linearly, and normalised only after averaging.
    Why it matters
    Sampling cost is what makes probabilistic error cancellation unusable at depth, so a large cut in it extends what a noisy processor can answer in the years before error correction.
    Be skeptical of
    22 data and 27 check qubits over six Trotter steps of an Ising model; the overhead saving is inferred from the model, and the first-order treatment need not survive higher error rates.
  4. 85E★Encapsulation epitaxy of air-stable monolayer superconducting films for quantum circuits and qubits
    Xudong Zheng, Sameia Zaman, Kenan Zhang et al. · 2026-09-16 · watchlist: William Oliver👍 👎

    Graphene or hBN encapsulation grows air-stable inch-scale monolayer NbSe2 at 0.7 nH per square kinetic inductance, patterned into circuits without oxidation.

    Explain
    In plain words
    They grew inch-scale sheets of a one-atom-thick superconductor underneath a protective lid, so the film survives air and can be patterned into circuits on an ordinary bench.
    How it works
    Graphene or hBN is laid on the substrate first and acts as both template and cover: NbSe2 grows epitaxially underneath it. The films keep a 1 K transition and are wired up with oxidation-free transfer and superconducting edge contacts.
    Why it matters
    0.7 nH per square of kinetic inductance in a material you can handle in air is a practical route to compact high-inductance elements, the sort of thing junction arrays are used for today.
    Be skeptical of
    A 1 K transition temperature is low for a circuit element, and the paper shows films and test structures rather than a working qubit.
  5. 84TEParametric two-qubit gates via Landau-Zener interference
    Simon Geisert, Albert Hertel, Soeren Ihssen et al. · 2026-09-14👍 👎

    Consecutive Landau-Zener transitions drive a two-qubit gate tunable across hundreds of MHz, demonstrated on both flux-qubit chiplets and monolithic transmons.

    Explain
    In plain words
    A two-qubit gate driven by sweeping the qubits through an avoided crossing twice, so that the interference between the two passes does the entangling.
    How it works
    Consecutive Landau-Zener transitions replace the usual resonant or dispersive drive, which lets the control frequency be tuned continuously over hundreds of MHz; shown both on coupled flux-qubit chiplets and on monolithic transmons.
    Why it matters
    The gate runs at the full coupling strength rather than a dispersive fraction of it, and a freely chosen drive frequency lets control pulses be multiplexed, which is exactly what wiring chiplets together needs.
    Be skeptical of
    Two platforms, but the abstract reports tunability and speed rather than gate fidelity; this is a mechanism demonstration, not yet a high-fidelity gate.
  6. 82ETemporal information processing on a 4,500-qubit quantum annealer
    Antonio Sannia, Roberto Menta, Pratik Sathe et al. · 2026-09-16👍 👎

    Reverse annealing across 4,500 qubits runs reservoir computing that forecasts chaotic time series, the largest quantum machine-learning experiment so far.

    Explain
    In plain words
    A 4,500-qubit annealer was used, untrained, as the dynamical system inside a machine-learning model that forecasts chaotic time series.
    How it works
    Reverse annealing lets the machine's own many-body dynamics scramble the input, and only a linear readout is fitted afterwards, so there is no expensive training loop over quantum parameters.
    Why it matters
    It is the largest quantum machine-learning experiment run so far, and nothing quantum is trained, which is a completely different scaling story from variational circuits.
    Be skeptical of
    Standard memory benchmarks and chaotic forecasting with no classical baseline quoted; 'largest' counts qubits, and says nothing about advantage over a cheap classical reservoir.
  7. 81TEUnified scaling of transmon sub-resonant parametric drive strength limits
    Jacob Repicky, Girish Kumbhar, Mingkang Xia et al. · 2026-09-11👍 👎

    Fourteen transmons fix the maximum sub-resonant drive amplitude as one function of frequency-to-anharmonicity ratio, bounding achievable parametric coupler rates.

    Explain
    In plain words
    Fourteen transmons pin down how hard you can drive one before the spectrum starts mixing the states you care about with the ones you do not.
    How it works
    The critical drive amplitude, measured across a wide spread of anharmonicities, collapses onto a single function of the ratio of qubit frequency to anharmonicity, and matches Floquet branch analysis and semiclassical simulation.
    Why it matters
    It turns 'drive as hard as you dare' into a number you can design against, and directly bounds the interaction rate a parametrically driven transmon coupler can deliver.
    Be skeptical of
    Sub-resonant drives on one device family; the relation is an empirical collapse backed by simulation, not a derived hard limit.
  8. 80TAnalytic leakage suppression with a single control field: fast two-qubit gates with tunable couplers
    Lukas Heunisch, Michael J. Hartmann, Aashish A. Clerk · 2026-09-17👍 👎

    A Magnus-derived pulse shape needs only one baseband flux channel, cutting coupler-ramp leakage in a fast iSWAP by three orders of magnitude.

    Explain
    In plain words
    A pulse-shape correction in the spirit of DRAG, but for hardware that has only one real control line, which nearly eliminates the leakage caused by ramping a tunable coupler.
    How it works
    The correction is derived analytically from a Magnus expansion rather than needing a second, quadrature channel to break time-reversal symmetry; it is tested in simulation on a baseband flux-pulsed iSWAP between transmons.
    Why it matters
    Baseband flux control has exactly one channel, which was thought to rule out DRAG-like tricks, so this lets couplers be ramped faster without paying for it in leakage.
    Be skeptical of
    Simulation only, at realistic but chosen device parameters; no hardware demonstration, and real flux lines add distortion the model does not carry.
  9. 76ECryogenic wafer probing below one Kelvin: Characterization of normal-metal Coulomb blockade thermometers at wafer scale
    Lassi Lehtisyrjä, Renan P. Loreto, Juho Luomahaara et al. · 2026-09-11👍 👎

    A 300 mm cryogenic wafer prober reads sub-700 mK electron temperatures across a full 150 mm wafer, opening high-throughput screening.

    Explain
    In plain words
    Chip temperature was measured at points across an entire wafer inside a cryogenic prober, instead of dicing the wafer and cooling a few chips at a time.
    How it works
    Normal-metal Coulomb blockade thermometers read temperature from first principles with no calibration; a 300 mm cryogenic prober reads them below 700 mK across a full 150 mm wafer.
    Why it matters
    Cooldown turnaround, not measurement, is the real bottleneck in circuit development, so wafer-level screening below 1 K would let a bad process run be rejected without spending days in a dilution fridge.
    Be skeptical of
    700 mK is two orders of magnitude above where qubits actually operate, so this screens fabrication quality, never coherence.
  10. 72ECoherent and ultra-low-power EDSR with a flopping-mode spin qubit in germanium
    Alexei Orekhov, Wonjin Jang, Pan Zhang et al. · 2026-09-17👍 👎

    Germanium flopping-mode hole spin qubit hits 99.76 percent single-qubit fidelity at 5 mT using only -52 dBm of drive power.

    Explain
    In plain words
    A single hole spin spread across two quantum dots in germanium was driven electrically at very low power and with high single-qubit fidelity.
    How it works
    Delocalising the spin over a double dot gives it both first-order protection from charge noise and a large electric dipole, so electric-dipole spin resonance works at 5 mT with -52 dBm reaching the device, some 25 dB below the usual figure.
    Why it matters
    Not a superconducting circuit, but the same fridge and the same wiring budget: drive power is what heats the mixing chamber, and a spin-photon interface would share your microwave hardware.
    Be skeptical of
    Single-qubit operations only, T2* is 1.4 us, and the low-power result depends on sitting at a particular magnetic-field sweet spot.
