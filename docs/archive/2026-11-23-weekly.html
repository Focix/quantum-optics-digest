  1. 58EQuantitative measurement of microwave power at millikelvin temperatures: Comparison between dispersively and directly coupled superconducting qubits
    Asen Lyubenov Georgiev, Christoph Kissling, Fabian Kaap et al. · 2026-11-16👍 👎

    Compares transmons sitting directly on the feedline with dispersively coupled ones as calibrated Rabi-frequency probes of on-chip microwave power at millikelvin.

    Explain
    In plain words
    They used qubits as power meters to find out how much microwave power actually reaches a chip inside the fridge.
    How it works
    A qubit's Rabi oscillation rate is proportional to the field driving it, so measuring that rate gives the field amplitude; they compare qubits hanging straight on the line with qubits reached through a readout resonator.
    Why it matters
    Every drive-strength, photon-number and attenuation estimate in cQED rests on knowing the power at the device; an in-situ calibration with a waveguide-coupled qubit is directly reusable.
    Be skeptical of
    Accuracy depends on knowing each qubit's coupling to the line, which itself comes from modelling or a separate fit; it is a calibration study, not new physics.
  2. 55TGeneration of coherent-state Schrödinger cat states in a two-photon Jaynes-Cummings model implemented with Josephson junctions
    Hiroo Azuma, William J. Munro, Kae Nemoto · 2026-11-19👍 👎

    Flux-qubit two-photon Jaynes-Cummings coupling estimate shows cat-state generation is limited mainly by loss of the SQUID's bosonic mode.

    Explain
    In plain words
    They work out whether a real superconducting circuit could make Schrödinger cat states of microwave light by trading photons two at a time with a qubit.
    How it works
    A flux qubit coupled to the plasma mode of a dc SQUID gives a two-photon Jaynes-Cummings interaction; they estimate its strength and simulate flux noise, qubit decay and photon loss.
    Why it matters
    Two-photon exchange is a direct route to cat states without the usual Kerr or parametric drives, and the noise budget tells you which part of such a device to improve first.
    Be skeptical of
    Pure numerics on one assumed device; the coupling estimate has not been measured, and the readout SQUID mode is a lossy host for a cat.

    An ensemble of qubits coupled collectively to one optomechanical resonator raises microwave-to-optical conversion efficiency, with pump heating setting an optimal pump.

    Explain
    In plain words
    They propose using many superconducting qubits together, rather than one circuit, to talk to a vibrating mechanical element that converts microwave signals to light.
    How it works
    The qubits share one mechanical mode, so their coupling adds up collectively; near half excitation the collective coupling grows almost linearly with qubit number while the optical pump heats the device.
    Why it matters
    Transduction is the bottleneck for linking fridges optically; a collective interface is a knob that existing piezo-optomechanical transducers do not use.
    Be skeptical of
    Theory only, and it assumes many identical, well-controlled qubits on the transducer; added noise from real pump heating may be worse than modelled.

  5. 48ESideband frequency comb generation phase diagram in a high-impedance superconducting resonator
    Terence Bretz-Sullivan, Rupert Lewis · 2026-11-16 · Applied Physics Letters👍 👎

    Maps when sideband combs appear around four-wave-mixing comb teeth in a high-impedance NbTiN resonator, giving a pump-power/frequency phase diagram.

    Explain
    In plain words
    They drive a simple superconducting resonator hard and map when it spits out a comb of evenly spaced frequencies, and when extra combs appear around each tooth.
    How it works
    The kinetic-inductance nonlinearity of a high-impedance NbTiN line causes four-wave mixing; higher-order mixing produces sidebands at sharp jumps in transmission, which they chart versus pump frequency and power.
    Why it matters
    Kinetic-inductance resonators are common in parametric amplifiers and detectors; knowing where they go unstable is practical for anyone pumping them.
    Be skeptical of
    Classical nonlinear optics in a microwave resonator, with no quantum measurements; the sideband mechanism is described rather than fully modelled.
  6. 45TTransient Synchronization and Entanglement through Shared Dissipation in Circuit QED
    Areeda Ayoub, Eric R. Bittner · 2026-11-16👍 👎

    Proposes two resonators coupled via SQUIDs to a shared lossy mode, predicting sign-tunable transient synchronization and dissipation-driven entanglement.

    Explain
    In plain words
    They propose making two microwave resonators lock to a common rhythm and become entangled by letting both leak into the same lossy mode.
    How it works
    Eliminating the fast lossy mode leaves correlated damping whose sign and size are set by the SQUID flux biases; unequal collective decay then pulls the two resonators in or out of phase.
    Why it matters
    Engineered shared dissipation is a standard cQED tool, and a flux-tunable sign gives a simple handle on collective decay.
    Be skeptical of
    Theory only; the entanglement is transient and the protocol needs good control of the SQUID couplings.

    Explain
    In plain words
    They show that quantum theory's mathematical structure follows from one rule about which correlations nature allows.
    How it works
    Assume every observable can be measured ideally (repeatable, not disturbing compatible ones); then demand that every correlation such a theory permits actually occurs. Only Hilbert space with the Born rule fits.
