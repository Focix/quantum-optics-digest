  1. 92E★Bosonic Error Correction with Fluxonium
    Shantanu R. Jha, Shoumik D. Chowdhury, Gabriele Rolleri et al. · 2026-09-22 · watchlist: William Oliver👍 👎

    Planar GKP memory stabilised by a heavy fluxonium with 451 us bit-flip time, logical lifetime up 1.59x.

    Explain
    In plain words
    They stored a protected quantum state in a small on-chip resonator and kept correcting it, so it survived about 60% longer than if left alone.
    How it works
    A heavy fluxonium, which almost never flips, drives an Archimedean spiral resonator; finite-energy grid states are prepared and refreshed by measurement-free correction cycles with fast fluxonium reset.
    Why it matters
    Bosonic codes have until now needed centimetre-scale 3D cavities and transmons whose own flips capped the gain; doing it entirely in planar circuitry puts this style of error correction on the same fabrication line as everything else.
    Be skeptical of
    The gain is a factor of 1.59 on one device, and a weakly coupled fluxonium buys its bit-flip protection with slower, more awkward control.
  2. 90EQuantum computational advantage in random-circuit sampling on IBM superconducting quantum computers
    Tigran Sedrakyan, Yuxuan Zhang, Hovnatan Karapetyan et al. · 2026-09-23👍 👎

    61 qubits of IBM Nighthawk r2 sample circuits estimated at a century of Frontier time, run through the ordinary cloud stack.

    Explain
    In plain words
    A commercial cloud machine ran a sampling task in 19 seconds that the authors estimate would take a top supercomputer over a hundred years.
    How it works
    Random circuits on 61 of 120 qubits are run to 36 cycles with native CZ gates; two independent fidelity estimators agree over two decades of decay, and the classical cost is bounded with a contraction-cost model checked against the Sycamore and Zuchongzhi networks.
    Why it matters
    The claim rests on a stock device with no benchmark-specific tuning, so the hardware quality that used to require a hero run is now what a queue gives you.
    Be skeptical of
    Classical-cost estimates for sampling have been walked back before, and the fidelity at the claimed depth is only 2.3e-3.
  3. 86TESoft decoding for quantum LDPC codes with experimental validation
    Arda Aydin, Edwin Tham, Nicolas Delfosse et al. · 2026-09-22👍 👎

    Soft beam-search decoder doubles logical lifetimes in five qLDPC memory experiments, pushing all of them past breakeven.

    Explain
    In plain words
    A decoder that also reports how sure it is lets you throw away the shots it distrusts, and what remains has far fewer errors.
    How it works
    Beam search over correction candidates reuses its own internal scores as the confidence metric, so no extra computation is needed; circuit-level simulations of five codes and a re-analysis of published trapped-ion memory runs quantify the gain.
    Why it matters
    Confidence-weighted decoding is a software change on syndrome data you already record, and it buys error suppression that would otherwise cost better qubits.
    Be skeptical of
    The suppression is bought with post-selection, so it helps memories and detected-error operations but does not by itself carry a deterministic computation.
  4. 84TDenser Planar Color Codes
    Noah Shutty · 2026-09-18👍 👎

    4.8.8 colour code in a brickwork layout needs half the qubits of the rotated surface code at equal distance.

    Explain
    In plain words
    A different tiling of the error-correcting patch protects a logical qubit just as well while using about half as many physical qubits.
    How it works
    The 4.8.8 colour code is laid out brickwork-style on a square lattice with nearest-neighbour gates, and twelve-layer periodic superdense CNOT circuits appear to keep the full code distance; circuit-level noise simulations check the scaling.
    Why it matters
    Qubit count is the scarce resource on a chip, so halving it at fixed distance changes what a given wafer can hold.
    Be skeptical of
    Distance preservation is observed numerically rather than proved, colour codes are harder to decode than surface codes, and nothing here has been built.
  5. 83EAdaptive thresholding for scalable measurement-based qubit reset
    Qian Cao, Unnati Akhouri, Samuel Vizvary et al. · 2026-09-21👍 👎

    Bayesian feedback on the full analog readout record resets a transmon to 99.44%, five at once to 98.78%.

    Explain
    In plain words
    Instead of deciding after each measurement with one fixed cutoff, the reset keeps a running estimate of the qubit's state and raises its bar as evidence accumulates.
    How it works
    An FPGA updates the Bayesian estimate from each dispersive readout and moves the feedback threshold, using the whole analog record rather than a thresholded bit.
    Why it matters
    Reset sits at the start of every circuit and every error-correction round, and this version finishes in a fixed time instead of the open-ended wait repeat-until-success imposes.
    Be skeptical of
    Demonstrated on one transmon and a five-qubit set; the fidelities are good but not yet at the 99.9% level deep error correction wants.
  6. 82TEPrecision and resource scaling of real-time flux distortion compensation for superconducting quantum control
    Qi Zhou, Zi-Hao Mei, Peng Duan et al. · 2026-09-23👍 👎

    FPGA filter undoes flux-line distortion in real time at 1.2 GSa/s, CZ fidelity matching the 99.57% software reference.

    Explain
    In plain words
    Flux pulses leave a long tail that spoils later gates; a filter running live on the control hardware cancels it as the waveform is generated.
    How it works
    The compensation impulse response is reconstructed by frequency-domain inversion and fitted with a compact IIR plus FIR pair, then parallelised with look-ahead so an FPGA can run it at full sample rate.
    Why it matters
    Dynamic circuits cannot pre-store every waveform, so distortion correction has to happen on the fly; the resource cost grows only logarithmically with the timescale you cover.
    Be skeptical of
    It matches the existing software correction rather than beating it, and the demonstration is a two-qubit CZ, not a processor-scale run.
  7. 81TExtracting Electromagnetic Bare Mode Couplings in Large Superconducting Quantum Processors
    Reza Molavi, Ebrahim Forati, Yaxing Zhang et al. · 2026-09-18👍 👎

    Four solver-based methods agree within 5% on transmon couplings to package modes across a 10x10 array.

    Explain
    In plain words
    Four different ways of computing how strongly each qubit talks to the metal box around it give answers that agree to within a few percent.
    How it works
    Avoided level crossing, energy participation ratio, induced electromotive force and impedance-matrix extractions are set up for commercial 3D electromagnetic solvers and compared on a 100-transmon array.
    Why it matters
    Package modes are a known source of unexplained loss and crosstalk on large chips, and design has lacked a coupling number anyone trusted; cross-validated methods make that number usable before fabrication.
    Be skeptical of
    Agreement among four calculations is not agreement with a measured device, and the test case is a simulated array.
  8. 80TReconfigurable bus-based quantum router for modular superconducting processors
    Benzheng Yuan, chaojie Zhang, Yangyang Fei et al. · 2026-09-24👍 👎

    Shared-bus SQUID router cuts median SWAPs up to 34% on 36-qubit circuits while running two CZ gates in parallel.

    Explain
    In plain words
    A switchable shared wire lets distant qubits interact directly instead of shuffling states along a grid, and two such interactions can happen at once.
    How it works
    Flux-tunable SQUID couplers connect interface qubits to two buses, with destructive interference suppressing the idle coupling; full-system Hamiltonian simulation gives parallel-gate errors near 1e-3 and hardware-aware compilation measures the circuit-level saving.
    Why it matters
    Fixed nearest-neighbour connectivity is what makes SWAP networks dominate compiled depth, and this is a concrete coupler-level answer that a compiler can actually see.
    Be skeptical of
    Simulation only, and the depth benefit is circuit-dependent: 20% for QAOA-MaxCut but negligible for the QFT.
  9. 79TThe Pinnacle Architecture with fixed connectivity of degree eight
    Paul Webster, Tom Peham, Lawrence Z. Cohen · 2026-09-18👍 👎

    Degree-eight fixed connectivity factors RSA-2048 in a month with about 120,000 physical qubits at 1e-3 error.

    Explain
    In plain words
    A published architecture is reworked so every qubit needs only eight fixed neighbours, which is something a fabricated chip can actually provide.
    How it works
    The Pinnacle layout is re-instantiated under fixed-connectivity constraints, and the resource count for factoring a 2048-bit integer is worked out for a 1e-3 error rate, 1 microsecond code cycle and 10 microsecond reaction time.
    Why it matters
    Cryptographically relevant factoring estimates have kept falling, and this one is stated in the currency superconducting chips are built in: fixed wiring degree and physical qubit count.
    Be skeptical of
    A resource estimate resting on assumed error rates and reaction times, with no device anywhere near 120,000 qubits.
  10. 77TOptimising Cryogenic Wiring for Microwave-frequency Millikelvin Quantum Processors
    Adrien Di Lonardo, Giorge Gemisis, Juan Pablo Dehollain et al. · 2026-09-23👍 👎

    Whole-fridge wiring model beats best-practice coaxial designs on device temperature and qubit capacity, questioning 0 dB attenuators.

    Explain
    In plain words
    The cables and attenuators going down a dilution fridge were optimised as one system rather than by inherited rules of thumb, and better configurations came out.
    How it works
    A whole-system numerical model spans heat load, attenuation and noise across the cable stack; a wide configuration sweep yields design principles and a web tool, framed by an analogy to low-noise amplifier chains.
    Why it matters
    Cooling power, not chip area, is what caps how many control lines a fridge can carry, so wiring choices set the qubit ceiling of a real system.
    Be skeptical of
    Modelling rather than measurement, and the authors stress the best configuration depends on the system context, so there is no single recipe to copy.
