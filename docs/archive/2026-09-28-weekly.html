  1. 62TAnalysis of Josephson junctions switching time distributions for the detection of single microwave photons
    A. S. Piedjou Komnang, C. Guarcello, C. Barone et al. · 2026-09-22👍 👎

    Kramers-model analysis turns a junction's switching-time histogram into a detection efficiency and a decision rule for rare single-photon events.

    Explain
    In plain words
    A calculation of how to tell, from the times at which a Josephson junction jumps to a voltage state, whether a faint microwave field contained photons at all.
    How it works
    The junction sits biased just below its critical current, so thermal noise alone makes it switch at random times; a photon pulse adds to that rate, and comparing the two switching-time distributions with Kramers escape theory gives the quantum efficiency.
    Why it matters
    Counting single microwave photons still has no detector as routine as an avalanche diode in the optical, and this is the statistics you need to claim a detection when the false-positive rate from thermal escape is comparable to the signal.
    Be skeptical of
    Cavity and line are taken as ideal and the source periodic, so the numbers bound what the switching statistics allow, not what a real absorber and its loss budget would deliver.

    Jeffrey Mohan, Jérémy Berroir, Filippo Borselli et al. · 2026-09-22👍 👎

    A cold-atom memory reshapes stored photons into arbitrary output waveforms at near-unity efficiency, over three orders of magnitude of pulse duration.

    Explain
    In plain words
    An atomic memory that swallows a single photon and spits it back out with whatever pulse shape you ask for, losing almost nothing in the process.
    How it works
    A high-optical-depth cold-atom ensemble stores the photon as a collective spin excitation; shaping the control field on write and read maps an arbitrary input envelope onto an arbitrary output envelope, and the high optical depth is what keeps efficiency near unity while the shapes change.
    Why it matters
    Linking unlike platforms fails on waveform mismatch, not on efficiency alone: a microwave-optical link hands over photons microseconds long to emitters that want nanoseconds. A memory that is also a programmable shaper removes that mismatch instead of engineering around it.
    Be skeptical of
    It works in the optical at an atomic transition wavelength, so a superconducting node still needs a transducer in front of it, and the demonstration reshapes single photons rather than multiphoton or entangled inputs.
  2. 84EOptical Schrödinger cat states generated using cavity Rydberg quantum photonics
    Hendrik Hegels, Mae Eichenberger, Gerhard Rempe et al. · 2026-09-23👍 👎

    Rydberg-blockade cavity entangles a polarisation qubit with a coherent-state superposition of size alpha^2 = 2.4, the largest such hybrid state measured.

    Explain
    In plain words
    They made a beam of light that is in a superposition of two different amplitudes, and entangled that superposition with the polarisation of a photon travelling elsewhere.
    How it works
    Light in a cavity filled with a Rydberg-blockaded atomic medium acquires a nonlinearity strong enough at the few-photon level to split a coherent state into two components; full tomography of the joint state then certifies the entanglement, and the size record comes from suppressing decoherence in the medium.
    Why it matters
    This is the optical counterpart of the cat-state work done with microwave cavities and transmons, and its size is set by how strong a photon-photon interaction you can get. Hybrid discrete-continuous entanglement is the resource for teleporting between qubit-encoded and field-encoded nodes.
    Be skeptical of
    alpha^2 = 2.4 is a record among hybrid entangled states but still a small cat: the two components overlap appreciably, and the Rydberg medium's decoherence is what caps it.

    Explain
    In plain words
    Single atoms held in a reconfigurable array talk strongly to light in a fibre cavity, at the wavelength telecom fibres carry best.
    How it works
    Ytterbium-171 atoms are trapped along a nanofiber cavity and coupled on the metastable 3P0 to 3D1 transition near telecom wavelengths; reflection spectroscopy of the single-sided cavity gives an internal cooperativity of 5.6 per atom and root-N collective scaling up to five atoms, while heating a fibre Bragg grating tunes the outcoupling rate in situ.

    A cryogenic metallo-dielectric antenna collects 97% of one molecule's emission, delivering 10^8 photons per second at 91.2% indistinguishability.

    Explain
    In plain words
    A single dye molecule in a mirror-like sandwich emits a hundred million photons a second, almost all of which go into the lens.
    How it works
    A planar metallo-dielectric antenna around a dibenzoterrylene molecule at cryogenic temperature redirects the dipole emission into a narrow cone, giving 97% collection into the first lens while the Fourier-limited transition keeps 91.2% two-photon indistinguishability and 97.7% single-photon purity.

    Explain
    In plain words

    How it works

    Explain
    In plain words
    A resonator wired to a transmission line at two places hears its own past output come back after a fixed flight time, and that delay is a tunable memory for computing with the dynamics.
    How it works

    Explain
    In plain words
    Swapping a clean laser pump for deliberately noisy light makes the photon pairs come out in far burstier clumps, without spoiling their usefulness as a single-photon source.
    How it works
    In spontaneous four-wave mixing the pair rate goes as pump intensity squared, so a fluctuating pump is weighted towards its bright moments; combined with bosonic bunching this amplifies the output correlations, measured in a silicon nitride microring as g2(0) rising to 7.58 with a 2.4-times longer correlation time, while the heralded value stays at 0.04.
