# C. Foundations of quantum mechanics — first principles and interpretations

Relevant: papers that change or sharpen what quantum mechanics says about the world, argued from first principles.

- Interpretations and their consequences: Everett / many-worlds, Bohmian and pilot-wave mechanics, QBism, relational QM, consistent histories, Copenhagen variants — when the paper makes a sharp claim, not a restatement.
- The measurement problem: collapse models (GRW, CSL, Diósi–Penrose), decoherence and quantum Darwinism, the quantum-to-classical transition, derivations or tests of the Born rule.
- No-go theorems and extended Wigner's-friend scenarios; observer-dependence of facts; Frauchiger–Renner-type arguments.
- Reconstructions of quantum theory from physical or information-theoretic axioms; generalized probabilistic theories; why complex amplitudes, why the tensor product.
- Contextuality, nonlocality and ontological models as statements about reality (psi-ontic vs psi-epistemic, PBR, Kochen–Specker, Leggett–Garg / macrorealism).
- Experiments that test foundations: macroscopic superpositions, collapse-model bounds, loophole-free or new-regime Bell and Leggett–Garg tests, gravity-induced entanglement, indefinite causal order.
- Time, causality and reference frames in quantum theory when the point is conceptual.

Score high: a new theorem or argument with a checkable conclusion, an experiment that rules something out, a clean derivation that explains why the theory has the structure it has. Extra weight for foundational tests that run on superconducting circuits or photons, which the reader knows how to build.

Score low: Bell inequalities or contextuality used only as a resource (device-independent QKD, randomness certification, self-testing engineering); purely mathematical results with no physical reading; reviews that restate the textbook; claims that quantum mechanics is wrong that do not engage the existing evidence.
